NeuralPLSI

NeuralPLSI models an outcome as g(X′β) + Z′γ. The chemical exposures (X, a mixture) enter through one weighted index X′β, with β of unit length and a positive first weight, and an unknown function g learned by a small neural network. The confounders (Z) enter linearly. The outcome can be continuous, binary (logistic) or a survival time (Cox). A pairs bootstrap gives standard errors and percentile intervals. Method: Do, Wang, Liu and Lee (2025), arXiv:2512.11593.

Everything runs on this device: the C++ engine is compiled to WebAssembly and runs in Web Workers in your browser. Data you open are not uploaded.

1Data

The simulation design of the paper: 8 chemical exposures (X) with correlation 0.3, three confounders (Z), β ∝ (1, 0.7, −0.5, 0.5, 0.3, −0.1, 0, 0) and γ = (1, −0.5, 0.5). The generator is seeded but does not reproduce the draws of the Python package.

Log triglyceride of 800 NHANES participants against 8 chemical exposures (X: α- and γ-tocopherol, retinyl stearate, retinol, two PCBs, a furan, trans-β-carotene), with age, sex and race as confounders (Z). Prepared as in reproduce/nhanes.py: exposures logged (except retinol) and standardized, race coded as indicators. Selecting it sets the activation to ELU and adds an intercept, as that script does.

Data: supplementary material of Wang et al. (2020), Environmental Health 19:96, licensed CC BY 4.0.

The file is read on this device and never uploaded. It needs a header row; commas, semicolons or tabs separate the fields.

Missing values and numbers

Missing values are empty fields and the strings pandas reads as missing, matched exactly (NA, not na): NA, N/A, n/a, NaN, nan, -NaN, -nan, NULL, null, None, <NA>, #N/A, #N/A N/A, #NA, 1.#IND, -1.#IND, 1.#QNAN and -1.#QNAN. In a text column None is a category, not a missing value; . and missing are never missing values, so a column of numbers that contains them is text. Each column's notes list the strings counted as missing (blank: an empty field).

Numbers use a point as the decimal mark; Inf and -Inf are numbers (non-finite). In a semicolon- or tab-separated file, a column may use decimal commas (1,5) if none of its values has a point. A value that cannot be a thousands number (0,125, 1,5) shows decimal commas for the whole column; 1,250,000 has thousands separators; if every comma value could be either (1,250), values with commas are text, and the column's notes say so. A text column cannot be a chemical exposure (X) or the outcome.

Column roles

Give each column one role. X: chemical exposures, combined into the index X′β inside g. Z: confounders, which enter linearly. One outcome (for Cox, the survival time and a 0/1 event column). Leave IDs and survey weights on ignore: the fit is unweighted. With the keyboard, the up and down arrows move between columns and the left and right arrows (or Space) change the role.

X and Z columns enter the model in the order you give them their role; X rows show their position (X1, X2, …), and β has a positive weight on X1. Each X row has a log box. A numeric Z column of codes, such as race coded 1 to 5, can be marked categorical: it then becomes indicators against its lowest value. Columns with 2 to 10 integer codes, other than 0/1, are suggested.

Chemical exposures (X)

Environmental concentrations are usually right-skewed: unlogged, a few high values can dominate the index. Log them with the log box of each X row, or Log-transform X: All. The log drops rows with a value of zero or below. Standardizing uses the mean and SD of the rows kept.

Outcome (continuous)

The log drops rows with an outcome of zero or below. Estimates are on the scale of the transformed outcome.

Rows with a missing or non-finite value in any chosen column are dropped; so are numbers beyond ±3.4 × 1038, the float32 range NeuralPLSI computes in. Text confounders, and numeric ones marked categorical, become indicator columns against their first level (at most 20 levels).

2Model

What these do

Family sets the loss: squared error, logistic likelihood or Cox partial likelihood. It follows the outcome chosen under Data: the simulated outcome, continuous for NHANES, or the outcome type of your CSV. Activation, hidden units and hidden layers shape the network for g. Weight decay is the L2 penalty on the network; larger values give a smoother g. Full-batch L-BFGS runs until the objective stops improving or max iterations; γ is then fitted exactly. g is anchored at g(0) = 0, so an intercept carries the outcome level (not identifiable for Cox). The seed sets the initialization and the bootstrap resamples.

SplinePLSI (Wang et al., 2020) replaces the network with a cubic B-spline with 5 knots and is fitted on the same data and bootstrap resamples, for comparison.

3Run

Fit uses all rows. Each bootstrap replicate resamples the rows with replacement and refits from a fresh initialization; standard errors are the SD of the draws and intervals their 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles. Replicates run in parallel, one per worker.
