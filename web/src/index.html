Neural Partially Linear Single-Index Model Demo

NeuralPLSI models an outcome as g(X′β) + Z′γ. The chemical exposures (X, a mixture) enter through one weighted index X′β, with β of unit length and a positive first weight, and an unknown function g learned by a small neural network. The confounders (Z) enter linearly. The outcome can be continuous, binary (logistic) or a survival time (Cox). A pairs bootstrap gives standard errors and percentile intervals. Method: Do, Wang, Liu and Lee (2025), arXiv:2512.11593.

Everything runs on this device: the C++ engine is compiled to WebAssembly and runs in Web Workers in your browser. Data you open are not uploaded.

Data source

The file is read on this device and never uploaded. It needs a header row; commas, semicolons or tabs separate the fields.

No file at hand?

Triglyceride of 800 NHANES participants against 8 chemical exposures (X: α- and γ-tocopherol, retinyl stearate, retinol, two PCBs, a furan, trans-β-carotene), with age, sex and race as confounders (Z). It opens as your data, with the columns set up as reproduce/nhanes.py does: exposures logged (except retinol) and standardized, log triglyceride standardized, sex and race coded as indicators; change any of them. It also sets the activation to ELU and adds an intercept, as that script does. Data: supplementary material of Wang et al. (2020), Environmental Health 19:96, licensed CC BY 4.0.

Missing values and numbers

Missing values are empty fields and the strings pandas reads as missing, matched exactly (NA, not na): NA, N/A, n/a, NaN, nan, -NaN, -nan, NULL, null, None, <NA>, #N/A, #N/A N/A, #NA, 1.#IND, -1.#IND, 1.#QNAN and -1.#QNAN. In a text column None is a category, not a missing value; . and missing are never missing values, so a column of numbers that contains them is text. Each column's notes list the strings counted as missing (blank: an empty field).

Numbers use a point as the decimal mark; Inf and -Inf are numbers (non-finite). In a semicolon- or tab-separated file, a column may use decimal commas (1,5) if none of its values has a point. A value that cannot be a thousands number (0,125, 1,5) shows decimal commas for the whole column; 1,250,000 has thousands separators; if every comma value could be either (1,250), values with commas are text, and the column's notes say so. A text column cannot be a chemical exposure (X) or the outcome.

The simulation design of the paper: 8 chemical exposures (columns x1 to x8) with correlation 0.3, three confounders (z1 to z3), β ∝ (1, 0.7, −0.5, 0.5, 0.3, −0.1, 0, 0) and γ = (1, −0.5, 0.5); the outcome is y (time and event for Cox). The columns below start as generated, and the results show the truth while they stay so. The generator is seeded but does not reproduce the draws of the Python package.

Columns and preprocessing

Give each column one role. X: chemical exposures, combined into the index X′β inside g. Z: confounders, which enter linearly. One outcome (for Cox, the survival time and a 0/1 event column). Leave IDs and survey weights on ignore: the fit is unweighted. X and Z columns enter the model in the order you give them their role; β has a positive weight on X1. With the keyboard, the up and down arrows move between columns and the left and right arrows (or Space) change the role.

Chemical exposures (X)

    No chemical exposures yet: give columns the X role under Columns.

    Environmental concentrations are usually right-skewed: unlogged, a few high values can dominate the index; each X's histogram shows its values as the model takes them, on the log scale when it is logged. Log-transform logs every X; clear the box of an X to leave it unlogged. The log drops rows with a value of zero or below. Standardizing uses the mean and SD of the rows kept.

    Confounders (Z)

      No confounders yet: give columns the Z role under Columns.

      Each numeric confounder has its own log and standardize boxes, set apart from the exposures'; the boxes above set them for every numeric confounder. A log drops rows with a value of zero or below; standardizing uses the mean and SD of the rows kept. A numeric confounder of codes, such as race coded 1 to 5, can be marked categorical: it then becomes indicators against its lowest value. Columns with 2 to 10 integer codes, other than 0/1, are suggested. Text confounders are always categorical, against their first level (at most 20 levels).

      Outcome

      The log drops rows with an outcome of zero or below. Estimates are on the scale of the transformed outcome.

      Models

      Choose the models to fit with Run on each; both use the family and seed below, the same data and the same bootstrap resamples.

      Family sets the loss: squared error, logistic likelihood or Cox partial likelihood. It follows the outcome type chosen under Data. The seed sets the initializations and the bootstrap resamples.

      NeuralPLSI

      NeuralPLSI settings
      What these do

      A small neural network learns g. Activation, hidden units and hidden layers shape it. Weight decay is the L2 penalty on the network; larger values give a smoother g. Full-batch L-BFGS runs until the objective stops improving or max iterations; γ is then fitted exactly. g is anchored at g(0) = 0, so an intercept carries the outcome level (not identifiable for Cox).

      SplinePLSI

      SplinePLSI settings
      What these do

      SplinePLSI (Wang et al., 2020) replaces the network with a cubic B-spline. Knots are placed at quantiles of the index (more knots, a more flexible g); the ridge penalty shrinks the spline and γ. β is found by BFGS over the unit sphere, for at most max iterations; the spline's g carries the outcome level.

      Run

      estimates only, no standard errors

      Fit with bootstrap fits the model on all rows, then refits it on B bootstrap resamples (rows drawn with replacement, each refit from a fresh initialization): standard errors are the SD of the draws and intervals their 2.5% and 97.5% quantiles. Replicates run in parallel, one per worker. When the setup has not changed since the last fit, it bootstraps that fit. The results open when the fit ends; the standard errors fill in when the bootstrap does.
